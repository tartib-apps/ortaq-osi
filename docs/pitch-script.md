## The value in one breath

Everyone should be able to say this:

- **Problem:** an apartment building in Kazakhstan has no shared, reliable record of what happens in it. The chairperson works blind and carries all the legal responsibility. Residents pay without seeing where the money goes, and decisions stall because meetings never reach quorum.
- **Solution:** Ortaq turns the building into one system where every action leaves a trace. Requests become tracked tasks with photo proof. Every expense carries its receipt, so the official report builds itself. Votes are legally signed from a phone.
- **Value:** chairpersons get their time back and stay compliant. Residents get transparency and problems fixed. Buildings can make decisions again.

## Speaker 1: The problem

**Slide 1**
Good afternoon. Our Project is Ortaq. In Kazakhstan, around 300,000 apartment buildings are now run by their own owners, through associations called OSIs. Ortaq is the platform that runs them: a web workspace for the OSI and mobile apps for residents and staff. One idea runs through everything we’ll show you today: in a building on Ortaq, everything is visible. Every request, every tenge, every vote leaves a trace. Let’s start with why that matters.

**Slide 2**
Start with the person who holds the building together: the chairperson. Since the OSI reform, one elected, usually unpaid person carries everything. Residents’ requests arrive in a WhatsApp chat and sink within an hour, with no status, no owner, no deadline. Receipts live in a pocket and contracts in a folder, so there’s nothing to show residents as proof. Every month, by the 20th, there’s a mandatory official report, compiled by hand. And any major decision needs a general meeting where quorum is counted by floor area, which is almost impossible to gather in person. The law makes the chairperson accountable for all of it, and gives them no tools.

**Slide 3**
Now the other side. Residents pay fees every month and have no idea where the money goes: not one receipt in sight. A complaint in the group chat disappears under hundreds of messages. And the meeting that decides how their money is spent happens without them. One more thing matters a lot: residents don’t have to use anything. They already have WhatsApp. So whatever we build has to prove its value from the very first screen. And look at the last message in that chat: “Where did the roof repair money go?” That question is the real problem.

**Slide 4**
Both problems have one root. The building has no shared, reliable, accessible state. The chairperson can’t prove what was done, and residents can’t check. That breeds distrust, unpaid fees and failed meetings. Another chat app won’t fix it, because this isn’t a communication problem. It’s a data problem.

## Speaker 2: The solution and the product

**Slide 5**
Let's now talk about our solution and product. Ortaq turns the building into one system where every action leaves a trace, visible to everyone it concerns. It works through four mechanisms. First, a resident’s request becomes a task with stages, an assignee and a photo report. Second, every expense carries its receipt and maps to a line of the official reporting form, so the monthly report builds itself. Third, votes are signed with a legally valid digital signature through eGov Mobile, and the minutes are generated automatically. And fourth, the legal routine (minutes, reports, references to the law) is prepared by the system, not by the chairperson at midnight.

**Slide 6**
This is the real product. On the left is the chairperson’s web workspace. Urgent requests are on top, like this burst pipe, with an AI hint and a one-click “assign a plumber”. On the right is everything waiting for a decision today, and below, how much of this month’s fees has been collected and what’s in the building’s accounts. In the middle is the resident app: fees due with a Kaspi payment button, the vote that needs them, and live progress on the leak in the basement. On the right is the worker app: today’s jobs, urgent first. Three roles, one shared state of the building.

**Slide 7**
Here is the flow that wins residents over. A resident reports a problem in three steps: where, which floor, a photo. AI checks whether it’s a duplicate and suggests a priority. The chairperson assigns someone in two taps, often from a phone while walking the building. The worker closes the job with a photo, even in a basement with no signal. And once the chairperson approves it, the resident sees the photo of the result. No request gets lost, and the resident can see that something was actually done.

**Slide 8**
The same flow on real screens. One: the resident picks where the problem is from large tiles, not a long form, and AI checks whether someone already reported it. Two: the chairperson sees new requests on top and assigns them in a couple of taps, from anywhere. Three: the worker closes the job with a photo, using big buttons made for gloves, and it works without signal. Four: the resident follows every stage until it’s fixed. That is the moment WhatsApp can’t compete with.

## Speaker 3: Money and decisions

**Slide 9**
Money is where trust is won or lost. In Ortaq, every expense is tied to a photo of the receipt and to a line of the official reporting form under Order No. 362. On the right you see one amount in three places: the receipt, the expense in the building budget, and the line in the report. So the resident’s dashboard and the official report are literally the same numbers; they match to the tenge. Receipts are fingerprinted, so the same receipt can’t be posted twice. And the report is ready to sign by the 20th, without a weekend of spreadsheets.

**Slide 10**
Here’s what that looks like. On the left, the chairperson adds an expense by photographing the receipt. AI reads it and fills in the form, and the highlighted fields show what it recognised. The expense category can only come from the official chart of accounts, so it lands in the right line of the report automatically. On the right is what the resident sees: the building’s budget, where every line opens down to the receipt and the act of completed work. Same data, two views. Nobody has to ask “where did the money go” any more.

**Slide 11**
Next, decisions. In-person general meetings rarely reach quorum, so important repairs stall for months. Ortaq runs the vote electronically, as the law allows under Article 44, or as a written poll under Article 43. Residents sign their ballot with their digital signature in eGov Mobile by scanning a QR code. Quorum, over 50 percent of floor area, is counted automatically. The legal threshold comes from the type of decision, so it’s impossible to end up with void minutes. The owners’ registry is frozen when the vote starts, and the minutes are generated and signed at the end.

**Slide 12** 
In the product, the chairperson creates a vote by choosing the type of decision. The legal threshold and the notice period fill in automatically, and the system won’t let them start a vote that would break the law. On the right, the resident opens the ballot on their phone, reads the materials, and signs with their digital signature. A decision that used to take months of door-knocking now takes days, and the minutes hold up legally.

## Speaker 4: AI, trust, business, close

**Slide 13**
On top of the platform sits AI, with one firm principle: AI prepares, people decide. It triages incoming requests, reads receipts, turns a chairperson’s voice note into assignments, compares contractor quotes, answers legal questions with citations, and writes a weekly summary for the whole building. But rejecting a request, approving an expense or signing minutes is always a human action, because legal responsibility stays with the chairperson. And the system works fully with AI switched off. AI saves hours; it never becomes a single point of failure.

**Slide 14**
Two examples. On the left is the legal assistant. It answers only with a reference to the specific article of law, and if it can’t find one, it honestly says so instead of guessing. On the right, the chairperson walks around the building and simply dictates what they see. AI splits the voice note into separate tasks and suggests who should do each one. Nothing is sent until the chairperson checks it. That’s the pattern everywhere: AI saves the time, the person keeps the decision.

**Slide 15**
We’re asking buildings to trust us with their money and their votes, so trust is built into the architecture. Every building’s data is isolated at the application, database and test level. A hash-chained audit log means transactions and ballots can’t be quietly rewritten, and residents can see the log. Personal data never reaches the AI. And ballots are secret even from the chairperson. No software can stop every abuse, but Ortaq makes it visible, and what can’t be hidden usually isn’t done.

**Slide 16**
The business model is simple: the OSI pays, and the whole building uses it. The chairperson and accountant work on the web, residents and workers on mobile. Pricing is 50 to 100 thousand tenge per month per building, depending on the number of apartments. For comparison, tools like eOSI charge about 20 tenge per apartment but cover billing only. Ortaq replaces billing, requests, reporting and voting in one product. The market: about 300,000 apartment buildings and roughly 36,000 OSIs and partnerships. Since September 2025 simple partnerships are abolished, which pushes even more buildings into OSIs, and every one of them needs exactly what we’ve built.

**Slide 17**
Where we are today. The platform is built: the foundation with identity verification through digital signature and the owners’ registry; requests and tasks; finance with receipts, Kaspi payments and the official report; legally valid voting; and web and mobile apps for every role. The one module still in progress is AI, and it’s deliberately last, because the system has to work without it. Once it’s done, we launch. After launch come Kaspi Pay merchant integration, direct eGov integration and the Kazakh-language release.

**Slide 18**
To sum up: Ortaq gives every building one shared truth. Chairpersons get their evenings back and stay on the right side of the law. Residents see where every tenge goes and get their problems fixed. And buildings can finally make decisions. Thank you for your attention. We’re happy to take your questions.

## Likely questions

- **Why would residents install another app?** They don’t have to: the web version works the same, joining is one link, and the first screen shows their fees and their building’s money.
- **What if the chairperson abuses access?** Transactions and ballots are immutable, the audit log is visible to residents, and ballots are secret from the chairperson.
- **Is an electronic vote legally valid?** Yes: Articles 43 and 44 allow written and electronic voting, ballots are signed with a digital signature through eGov, and thresholds come from the law.
- **What happens if the AI is wrong or switched off?** Nothing breaks. AI only drafts; people confirm every action, and the platform runs fully without it.
